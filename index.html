Why score your songs?

Know the shape of your song list.

Most worship teams sense whether a set leans toward proclamation or prayer. Scoring every song lets you see the balance, both for the whole library and for each Sunday.

  • Plan with intention. Open with a declaration, move toward response, and close in prayer, on purpose.
  • Spot gaps. Find out whether your most-used songs lean heavily one way.
  • Filter in Planning Center. The scores are ordinary song tags, so they work in song search and reports.
  • Build shared language. One rubric gives your team a consistent way to talk about songs.

How it works

Four steps, all from one simple menu.

Double-click Start Tagger, pick a number, and follow the prompts. If you can fill in a spreadsheet, you can do this.

1

Set up once

Create five score tags in Planning Center and connect the tool with a key. Its instructions walk you through it.

About 30 min, once
2

Make a scoring sheet

Export your songs from Planning Center. The tool turns the export into a simple spreadsheet, for every song or just the lists you choose.

5 min
3

Score each song

Type a 1–5 next to each song using the one-page rubric, or let Claude score them and review only the ones it's unsure about.

30 min – 2 hrs
4

Send to Planning Center

The tool sends 3 songs first so you can check them, then sends the rest. Every other tag on each song is kept.

About 10 min

Built to be safe

Your library stays intact.

What the tool does

  • Changes only the 1–5 score tag on each song. Lists, themes, seasons, and every other tag are kept.
  • Sends 3 songs first and waits until you've checked them in Planning Center.
  • Is safe to run again: songs that already have the right score are skipped.
  • Keeps a record of every song it sends in a log file.
  • Includes a practice mode with made-up songs that changes nothing.

What stays with you

  • Your Planning Center key is saved only on your computer, and you can turn it off anytime in Planning Center.
  • Song exports and lyrics stay on your computer and are never included in the project files.
  • Claude reads lyrics only to choose a score and is instructed never to reproduce them.
  • The code is open source, so anyone can read exactly what it does.

Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need to know how to code?

No. You double-click a file, choose numbers from a menu, and fill in a spreadsheet. The Admin Guide is written for office admins and volunteers, and covers installing Python (a free program the tool needs) on Mac and Windows.

Will it change anything else in Planning Center?

No. It changes only the 1–5 score tag on each song and keeps every other tag. It also sends 3 songs first so you can check them before it does the rest.

Do I need Claude or any other AI?

No. You can score every song yourself with the printed rubric. Claude is optional and makes the job faster: it scores the list and flags only the songs it's unsure about for you to review.

What does it cost?

The tool is free and open source. You need a Planning Center Services account that can edit songs. If you choose to use Claude, the usual Claude plan applies.

What happens to our song lyrics?

Lyrics come from your own Planning Center export and stay on your computer. They're shared with Claude only if you choose that route, and Claude is instructed to use them only to choose a score, never to reproduce them. The project files never include any lyrics.

Can we use different labels or a different scale?

Yes. Planning Center's tag names can say anything as long as each starts with its number, 1 through 5. You can also adapt the rubric to your context. Just make sure everyone on your team uses the same one.

What about songs we add later?

Export again and choose to leave out songs that already have a score. Your new scoring sheet will include only the new songs.

Is this made by Planning Center?

No. It's an independent tool built by a church worship team and shared for others to use. It uses Planning Center's official API.

Ready to try it?

Start with the practice mode. It runs the whole process with made-up songs and changes nothing in Planning Center.
