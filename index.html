Why tag your songs?

For God's Glory and our Good

When the church sings, we sing to God, bringing Glory to His name, and in doing so we sing for the benefit of each other. Congregational worship does both.

5

Vertical

Sung to God

Songs and expressions that ascribe worth to God. Their direction is upward, and they're often a response to the revelation of Jesus. These songs echo what heaven is already doing.

For example: Holy, Holy, Holy · Great Are You, Lord

1

Horizontal

Sung about God, edifying one another

Songs about God, His Word, and His works. They're declarative: they proclaim truth and doctrine, and they edify the body as we sing that truth together.

For example: Christ Our Hope in Life and Death · Is He Worthy?

Which should we choose? Both.

The right balance isn't always the middle. When the moment calls for ascribing glory to God, we lean vertical. When the body needs to be reminded of eternal truth, we lean horizontal.

Either way, singing together is never only personal. Scripture says we address one another as we sing to the Lord (Ephesians 5:19–21), and that everything we do when we gather is for edifying one another (1 Corinthians 14:26). Greg Gilbert of 9Marks applies this to singing: sing even the songs that aren't your favorites, and sing them wholeheartedly, because someone beside you may be deeply strengthened by them. That's true whether you're on a microphone, holding a guitar, or running slides from the back.

From revelation to response

Worship often moves from revelation to response. Many churches shape a service to retell the gospel, and that flow tends to move from horizontal toward vertical:

  1. God Who He is: His nature and attributes
  2. Us Our sin, our condition, and our need
  3. Christ His atoning work: our assurance and salvation
  4. Response How we answer all of it

Where tagging helps

A directional rating doesn't rank a song. A 1 isn't better or worse than a 5. It simply names which way the song speaks, so your team can:

  • plan a service's flow from revelation to response on purpose, not by feel alone
  • notice when a season, a series, or your most-used songs lean heavily one way
  • filter and report on it in Planning Center, because the ratings are ordinary song tags
  • talk about songs with shared language across musicians, tech, and leadership

Planning tools have their limits. Whatever we do, in word or deed, we do in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to the Father through Him (Colossians 3:17). Good intentions and careful planning don't make our worship acceptable; as Harold Best reminds us, only God, seeing faithfully offered work as perfect in Christ, does that.

Further reading: Harold M. Best, Unceasing Worship · Greg Gilbert on congregational singing at 9Marks

Opening it the first time

Your computer will warn you once. Here's what to click.

Apple and Microsoft warn you about any app that isn't registered with them, which costs a yearly fee. This free app isn't registered yet, so you'll see a warning the first time you open it. After that it opens normally.

On a Mac

  1. Open the downloaded file and drag HV Song Tagger into your Applications folder.
  2. Double-click HV Song Tagger in Applications. A box says Apple could not verify it. Click Done, not Move to Trash.
  3. Open the Apple menu, choose System Settings, then Privacy & Security.
  4. Scroll down to Security. Next to the message about HV Song Tagger, click Open Anyway.
  5. In the box that appears, click Open Anyway again, then enter your Mac password or use Touch ID.

If you don't see Open Anyway, double-click the app again, then go back to Privacy & Security. On macOS 14 or older, you can instead Control-click the app, choose Open, and click Open.

On Windows

  1. Double-click the downloaded HV-Song-Tagger-Windows-Setup file.
  2. If a blue Windows protected your PC box appears, click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Follow the installer, then open HV Song Tagger from the Start menu.

Is it safe? The warning means the app isn't registered with Apple or Microsoft. It doesn't mean anything was found wrong with it. Every line of the code is public on GitHub for anyone to read. Download it only from this page, not from a copy someone sends you.

Built to be safe

Your library stays intact.

What the app does

  • Changes only the 1–5 directional rating tag on each song. Lists, themes, seasons, and every other tag are kept.
  • Sends 3 songs first and waits until you've checked them in Planning Center.
  • Is safe to run again: songs that already have the right rating are skipped.
  • Lets you save a record of every song it sends.
  • Includes a practice mode with made-up songs that changes nothing.

What stays with you

  • Your Planning Center key goes only to Planning Center. If you ask the app to remember it, it's kept in your computer's password storage.
  • Lyrics come straight from your Planning Center account and stay on your computer, unless you choose to give them to an AI.
  • The AI prompt asks it to use lyrics only to choose a directional rating, and never to repeat them.
  • The code is open source, so anyone can read exactly what it does.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is a vertical song better than a horizontal one?

No. Both directions are biblical, and healthy corporate worship needs both. A directional rating describes how a song speaks, not its quality or its worth. A church may lean one way for a season, and that's fine. The point is to choose on purpose.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. You download the app, paste in a key from Planning Center, and click a directional rating for each song. There's nothing else to install.

How do I set up the tags in Planning Center?

You only do this once. When you connect, the app looks for these tags and shows you the same steps if they're missing.

1. Open the tag editor

  1. In Planning Center, open Services, then Songs.
  2. Click Actions, then Edit Tags.
  3. Stay on the Song tab.

2. Add the group and its five tags

  1. Click Add Tag Group.
  2. Name it Horizontal Vertical Scale. Leave Allow multiple selections off, then click Save.
  3. In the new group, add these five tags. Each one must start with its number:
    • 1 - Horizontal
    • 2 - Mostly Horizontal
    • 3 - Blend
    • 4 - Mostly Vertical
    • 5 - Vertical
Will it change anything else in Planning Center?

No. The app changes only the 1–5 directional rating tag on each song and keeps every other tag. It also sends 3 songs first so you can check them before it does the rest.

Do I need Claude or any other AI?

No. You can tag every song yourself with the printed rubric. AI is optional and makes the job faster: Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini suggests a directional rating for every song and flags only the songs it's unsure about for you to review.

Why does my computer warn me about it?

Apple and Microsoft show a warning for programs that aren't registered with them, which costs a yearly fee. This free app isn't registered yet. Here's how to open it anyway, which you only have to do once.

What does it cost?

The app is free and open source. You need a Planning Center Services account that can edit songs. If you choose to use AI, your usual plan for it applies.

What happens to our song lyrics?

The app reads lyrics from your own Planning Center account, and they stay on your computer. They're shared with an AI only if you choose that route, and the prompt asks it to use them only to choose a directional rating, never to repeat them.

Can we use different labels or a different scale?

Yes. Planning Center's tag names can say anything as long as each starts with its number, 1 through 5. You can also adapt the rubric to your context. Just make sure everyone on your team uses the same one.

What about songs we add later?

Open the app again and leave out songs that already have a directional rating. Only the new songs will be on your list.

Is this made by Planning Center?

No. It's an independent app built by a church worship team and shared for others to use. It uses Planning Center's official API.

Ready to try it?

Start with the practice mode. It runs the whole process with made-up songs and changes nothing in Planning Center.
